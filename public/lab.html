The lab

We test our recipes like scientists, because you have to.

A frozen drink machine is a physics problem wearing a party hat. Too little sugar and you get a brick. Too much and it never freezes at all. So we measure things, write them down, and keep the results — including the ones that went wrong.

The one thing that decides everything

Sugar is not just the sweetener in a slushie. It is the antifreeze. Dissolved sugar lowers the temperature at which water freezes, which is the only reason a slush is slushy instead of solid. Take the sugar out and you do not get a healthier drink — you get an ice cube.

Why the sugar-free version is hard

This is the part that surprises people, and it is the reason we test before we sell anything. How sweet something tastes and how much it stops water freezing are two completely different properties. They only travel together in ordinary sugar because that is what everyone is used to.

Sort this table two ways

Tap Freezing power, then tap Sweetness. The order changes completely — and that is the whole point. Sugar is the reference at 100.

Read it sideways. Salt has no sweetness at all and nearly six times sugar's freezing power. Sucralose is hundreds of times sweeter than sugar and does almost nothing to the freezing point — which is exactly why a drink sweetened only with it would taste sweet and freeze into a brick. That is a real prediction, and we are going to film it failing.

Colour without dye

A lot of parents would rather skip Red 40 and Blue 1. The honest answer is that natural colour is harder, not impossible — and which plant you pick depends on how sour the drink is.

Sour drinks like anthocyanins

Hibiscus, black carrot and purple sweet potato hold their colour in an acidic drink. Slush is acidic, so these are the right family.

Beetroot is the wrong one

It wants a much less sour drink and it fades in sunlight. It looks perfect in the jug and disappointing an hour into a party in the sun.

Cold helps a lot

Natural colour lasts almost twice as well cold as it does warm. A frozen tank is a good place for it. A sunny table is not.

The hard one: blue raspberry

Blue raspberry is a flavour, not a fruit — there is nothing naturally blue to make it from. The one real answer is butterfly pea flower, which steeps deep blue and then turns pink-purple the moment you add lemon. In the cup. In front of whoever is holding it. We are testing it.
