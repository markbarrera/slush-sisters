For Everest

This page is for our friend Everest, who runs EEZ Freeze in Wimberley. He is the reason Slush Sisters exists — we saw his business and wanted to do our own version up in Lakeway.

We are not competitors. We are 45 minutes apart and we send each other business. This is everything we have tried so far that worked, written down so he can use whatever is useful and skip whatever is not.

We will keep adding to it as we learn more.

Your website

Your site looks clean and it works. These are things we added to ours that made a difference.

Big wins
  • Add an online booking form. Right now people have to call, text, or email to book. A form on the site lets someone book at 11 pm on a Tuesday without waiting for a reply. It also captures their info in one place so nothing gets lost in a text thread. You do not need anything fancy. Here are the exact fields we use: name, email, phone, event date, event time, event address (so you know if it is in your delivery range), which two flavors they want (dropdown from your menu), "anything else we should know" (free text), and "how did you hear about us" (also free text, not a dropdown — the specific answer like "Jenny at the Wimberley pool" is more useful than a category like "word of mouth"). You can build this with Google Forms for free, or Tally.so is another free option that looks nicer. Either one emails you when someone submits.
  • Tell your story on the site. Your site says what EEZ Freeze does but not who you are. People book a kid-run business partly because of the kid. The fact that you are 11 and started this yourself is the most interesting thing about your company, and it is not on your website right now. Add a section about yourself — how you got the idea, how old you were when you started, what you have learned. One or two paragraphs and a photo of you at an event. This is the thing people share.
  • Add real photos from events. Photos of the machine at a real party, with real people holding drinks, convert way better than stock images or product shots. They show what the experience actually looks like. Ask customers if you can take a few photos during setup. Even phone photos work. Show the machine set up in a backyard, a kid holding a blue raspberry slushie, the table with cups ready to go.
SEO (showing up on Google)
  • Set up a Google Business Profile. When someone searches "frozen drink machine rental Wimberley" on Google, a Business Profile is what shows the map listing with your phone number, reviews, and a link to your site. Without one, you are invisible on Google Maps. This is free and it is probably the single highest-value thing on this list. Open Google Maps on your phone, tap your profile picture, tap "Your Business Profile," and follow the steps. Business name: EEZ Freeze. Category: "Party equipment rental service." Set it as a service-area business (no storefront address shown). For service areas, add: Wimberley, San Marcos, Kyle, Buda, New Braunfels, Canyon Lake, Blanco, and Dripping Springs. Business description: "Frozen drink machine rentals in Wimberley and the Texas Hill Country. Delivery, setup, and pickup included. Slushies for kids, frozen cocktails for grown-ups. Run by Everest, age 11." Google will verify you by phone or postcard — phone is instant if they offer it, postcard takes about a week.
  • Add a meta description to your site. A meta description is the two lines of text that show under your site name in Google search results. Without one, Google picks a random sentence from your page. With one, you control what people read before they click. In the <head> of your HTML, right after the <title> tag, paste this line exactly:
    <meta name="description" content="Frozen drink machine rentals in Wimberley and the Texas Hill Country. Delivery, setup, and pickup included. Slushies for kids, frozen cocktails for grown-ups. Run by Everest, age 11.">
  • Create a separate page for each town you serve. A page called "Frozen Drink Machine Rental in San Marcos" ranks for people searching those exact words in San Marcos. Your homepage cannot rank for every town. We have six of these and they bring in real traffic. Here are the pages we would make if we were EEZ Freeze, in priority order:
  1. 1. Wimberley — your home base. You already rank here but a dedicated page with "Frozen Drink Machine Rental in Wimberley, TX" in the title, a meta description, and FAQ schema will lock it down.
  2. 2. San Marcos — 15 minutes south, Texas State University campus, college parties plus family events. This is probably your biggest untapped market. Title: "Frozen Drink Machine Rental in San Marcos, TX."
  3. 3. Kyle — 20 minutes east, one of the fastest-growing cities in Texas, lots of new neighborhoods with young families throwing birthday parties. Title: "Frozen Drink Machine Rental in Kyle, TX."
  4. 4. Buda — 25 minutes northeast, same growth story as Kyle. Title: "Frozen Drink Machine Rental in Buda, TX."
  5. 5. New Braunfels — 30 minutes southeast, big tourist and river town. Lots of summer parties, Gruene Hall area, float trips. Title: "Frozen Drink Machine Rental in New Braunfels, TX."
  6. 6. Canyon Lake — 25 minutes south, lake community with summer parties very similar to the Lake Travis crowd we serve. Title: "Frozen Drink Machine Rental in Canyon Lake, TX."
  7. 7. Blanco — 25 minutes west, small Hill Country town but enough events to be worth a page. Title: "Frozen Drink Machine Rental in Blanco, TX."
  8. 8. Dripping Springs — 20 minutes northwest. We also serve Dripping Springs from our side, so this one overlaps. That is fine — we can both have a page and let the customer pick whoever is closer or available. No need to avoid it.

Each page needs: a title with the town name and "Frozen Drink Machine Rental," a meta description (one sentence about what you do there), a headline, one or two paragraphs about what parties look like in that area, your pricing, and a link to book. That is it — ours are about 400 words each. Write something real about the area, not generic filler.

  • Add structured data (schema markup). This is code in the page that tells Google exactly what your business is — name, location, service area, price, FAQs. Google uses it to show rich results (star ratings, price, FAQ dropdowns) instead of just a plain link. This one is more technical. If you are building the site yourself, search "LocalBusiness schema markup" and add a JSON-LD block in the <head>. If someone is helping you with the site, ask them to add it.
  • Add Open Graph tags so links look good when shared. When someone shares your site link on Facebook, iMessage, or Instagram, the OG tags control what image and text show up in the preview. Without them, the link looks blank or pulls a random image. Paste these lines in the <head> of your HTML, after the meta description:
    <meta property="og:title" content="EEZ Freeze — Frozen Drink Machine Rentals in Wimberley, TX"> <meta property="og:description" content="Frozen drink machine rentals in Wimberley and the Hill Country. Delivery, setup, pickup included."> <meta property="og:image" content="https://eezfreeze.com/your-og-image.png"> <meta property="og:image:width" content="1200"> <meta property="og:image:height" content="630">For the image, make a 1200×630 pixel graphic — your EEZ Freeze logo centered on a solid background color. Canva can do this for free in about two minutes. Save it as a PNG, upload it to your site, and put the URL in the og:image tag.

Google Search Console

  • Set up Google Search Console. It is free and it shows you exactly what people are typing into Google when your site shows up, how often they click, and which pages rank. Without it, you are guessing what search terms matter. Go to search.google.com/search-console, add your site as a "Domain" property, and verify with a DNS TXT record (your domain registrar has instructions for this). It takes a few days to start showing data.

Analytics (knowing what is happening on your site)

  • Add basic analytics so you know how many people visit. Right now you have no way to know how many people look at your site, which pages they visit, or where they come from. Even simple numbers — 50 visitors this week vs. 200 — tell you whether what you are doing is working. We use PostHog. Here is how to set it up: go to posthog.com, sign up with your email, create a project called "EEZ Freeze," and it will give you a script tag — a few lines of code that start with <script>. Paste that right before the </head> tag in your HTML. That is it. Once it is in, you will see a dashboard showing how many people visited, which pages they looked at, where they came from, and what they clicked. The free tier handles up to a million events a month, which is way more than either of us will ever hit.
  • Use UTM-tagged links when you post on social media. A UTM tag is extra text on the end of a URL that tells your analytics where the click came from. Without it, you see "someone visited my site" but not "someone visited because of the Instagram post I made on Tuesday." When you post on Instagram, share this link instead of plain eezfreeze.com:
    eezfreeze.com?utm_source=instagram&utm_medium=socialFor Facebook:
    eezfreeze.com?utm_source=facebook&utm_medium=socialFor TikTok:
    eezfreeze.com?utm_source=tiktok&utm_medium=socialSave these in your phone’s notes so you can copy-paste them. Your analytics will then show exactly how many visitors came from each platform.

Social media

We are still figuring this out ourselves. Here is what the research says and what we have seen work.

  • Post Reels, not just photos. Instagram and TikTok both push video way harder than static photos. A 15-second Reel of you setting up the machine at a party reaches 2–3 times more people than a photo of the same setup. The easiest Reel to make is a setup timelapse: prop your phone up, start recording, set up the machine and the table, speed it up to 15 seconds. Shoot one at every rental.
  • Show yourself, not just the product. We looked at a bunch of similar businesses on Instagram. The ones that grow fastest are the ones where you see the person running it. An 11-year-old running a real business is the interesting part — slushies are just the vehicle. People share the story, not the drink. Behind-the-scenes content: loading the machine into the car, counting the money after a rental, testing a new flavor, arguing with your parents about the business. The less polished, the better.
  • Post in your local Facebook groups. For a local service business, neighborhood Facebook groups convert better than Instagram or TikTok. The people in those groups are the ones actually booking parties in your area. Search Facebook for these kinds of groups and join them: "Wimberley Community," "Wimberley Texas Moms," "San Marcos Moms," "Kyle Texas Community," "Buda Texas Community," "New Braunfels Moms Group," and any neighborhood-specific groups in the subdivisions near you. Post when you have an opening, share a photo from a recent event, answer when someone asks about party ideas. Do not spam — be helpful and be real. One post in a neighborhood group with 3,000 members is worth more than an Instagram post that reaches 200 strangers.
  • Not everything has to be selling. If every post is "book a machine," people stop following. Roughly half of what you post should just be you — business lessons, school stuff, behind the scenes, funny things that happened. The people who follow you for that are the ones who eventually book.

Product ideas

These are things we have done or are thinking about. Take whatever sounds good.

  • Add a candy garnish to every cup. This is our thing and you are welcome to steal it. A piece of candy on the rim that matches the flavor costs almost nothing per cup and it is the thing people photograph and post. It is the most shared part of the experience. For your four flavors: Blue Raspberry → blue Jolly Rancher or blue gummy bear. Green Apple → green apple ring or green Jolly Rancher. Watermelon → Sour Patch Watermelon. Pina Colada → a gummy pineapple ring or a small pineapple wedge if you want to go fresh. Buy in bulk from Sam’s Club or Amazon — a bag that does 50 cups costs about $4.
  • Expand the flavor menu. You have four flavors right now (Blue Raspberry, Green Apple, Watermelon, Pina Colada). More options mean more reasons for repeat customers and more things to post about. We have sixteen and it gives people something to talk about when they are picking. Flavors we would add next if we were you: Strawberry (the most popular kids’ flavor by far), Mango (works great as a cocktail mixer too), Lemonade (everyone likes it, easy to make), and Cherry Limeade (the Sonic drive-through flavor — people get excited about it). Test each one in a small batch first to make sure it freezes right in your machine. If it comes out too icy or too soft, adjust the sugar ratio — we post our experiments at slushsisters.com/lab if you want to see how we test.
  • Consider a premium fresh-fruit tier. We are working on a "Fresh Press" option — real limes, agave, no artificial dye — at a higher price point. The Hill Country / wine country crowd will pay more for something that feels handmade. Wimberley seems like exactly the right market for this. Start with a simple fresh limeade or lemonade recipe. Test whether it freezes properly in your machine before you sell it — low-sugar mixes can freeze too hard.
  • Offer themed or named packages. Instead of "pick two flavors," offer a "Birthday Bash" (strawberry + blue raspberry + candy bar) or a "Pool Party" (watermelon + pina colada). Named packages make the decision easier for the customer and give each setup its own identity for content.

Business stuff

  • Get business insurance. Schools, HOAs, and corporate events will not book you without it. Even backyard parties — if something goes wrong with the machine at someone’s house, insurance is what protects your family. It is boring but it is real.
  • Put your pricing on the site. Your pricing is already on your site, which is good — a lot of businesses hide it. The clearer you make it (one price, everything included, no surprises), the more people book without needing to call first.
  • Track where every booking comes from. Knowing that five bookings came from the Wimberley Facebook group and zero came from Instagram tells you where to spend your time. Without tracking, you are guessing. The simplest version: add "How did you hear about us?" to your booking process (call, text, or form). Write it down for every single booking. Review it once a month.

We will update this page as we figure things out. If you try something from this list and it works (or does not), let us know and we will add it.
