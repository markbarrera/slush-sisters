The longer version

Slush Sisters is a frozen drink machine rental company in Lakeway, Texas, run by two sisters — Harper, 11, and Finley, 8. They rent one two-tank machine for parties across the Lake Travis area at $250, everything included, and they come back the next morning to clean it themselves.

What makes it unusual is not the machine. It is that they treat it like a science problem. They measure the sugar in every batch with a refractometer, because sugar is what stops a slush freezing solid, and they publish the numbers and the failed experiments on their own website. They have written down seven rules they can be caught breaking, each one with a line saying what it costs them to keep.

They also published a rule about alcohol before anybody asked them to: they make the frozen mix, which has none in it, and a grown-up adds their own spirits after the girls have left.

What they can actually talk about

These are the ones where an 11-year-old and an 8-year-old will give you a real answer rather than a rehearsed one.

  • Why sugar is antifreeze. Take the sugar out of a slushie and you do not get a healthy slushie, you get an ice cube. They have made one. They can show you the number where it happens.
  • Blue raspberry is not a fruit. Somebody made it up, so there is nothing blue to squeeze. Their answer is a flower that turns pink when you add lemon, in the cup, while you are holding it.
  • Running a business with your sister. Who decides, what they argue about, what each of them is actually better at.
  • The things they got wrong. They keep a public list. It includes a calculator on their own site that told customers their best recipe was a block of ice.
  • Why they will not touch the alcohol, and why they wrote that down before anyone asked.
  • What it costs. They know their numbers — what a machine costs, what a party costs them, why the price does not change on a hot weekend.
