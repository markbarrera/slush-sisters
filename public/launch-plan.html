They deliver to your house, set up the machine with candy on every cup, and pick it up the next day. $250 for the whole day. They do both kid-friendly slushies and frozen cocktails for adults — your choice. Serving Lakeway, Bee Cave, Steiner Ranch, and the Lake Travis area. Their website: slushsisters.com/go/fb"

What photo to include

Attach 2-3 photos: the hero shot (both girls with the machine), the candy cup close-up, and the party setup. Facebook posts with photos get 2-3x more engagement than text-only.

One post per group. Don't post in 5 groups on the same day — it looks spammy. Space them out: one group per day over a week. And some groups have rules about self-promotion — read the pinned post before posting. If they say "Business posts on Fridays only," wait until Friday.

When someone replies or asks a question

Reply quickly. Be helpful, not salesy. If someone asks "do you do margaritas?" say "Yes! We have a frozen margarita mix — you add your own tequila. We can also do kid-friendly flavors in the other tank. slushsisters.com/flavors"

Nextdoor is neighbors talking to neighbors. It's less formal than Facebook groups. Post in the "For Sale & Free" or "Recommendations" section.

Post text: "Neighbor recommendation (from us!): My girls started Slush Sisters — they rent frozen drink machines for parties. They're 11 and 8, they deliver in the Lakeway/Bee Cave area, and they put candy on every cup. $250 for the whole day, delivery and pickup included. slushsisters.com/go/nd"

Include the hero photo. Nextdoor surfaces posts to nearby neighborhoods automatically.

How to start

Constraint: Lakeway/Westlake households are privacy-sensitive. Photos of their house, kids, or guests are usually a hard no. Plan for content from these events to be the girls only. Ask before every shot.

🔄 Phase 5 — Keep Going

Ongoing

After the launch push, the work shifts to two things: posting content and following up. Half your posts should have nothing to do with slushies — just be yourselves.

  • Post 2-3 times per week on Instagram/TikTok Harper and Finley
  • Reply to every Facebook group question about party rentals Dad
  • Check the dashboard weekly Anyone
  • After every booking: ask for a photo you can share Harper or Finley
  • After 10 bookings: review what worked Everyone

Aim for 2-3 posts per week. This is a suggested rhythm, not a rule. Skip a week if you're busy — consistency matters more than frequency.

Weekly rhythm

  • Monday or Tuesday: A "just us" post — trend, sister challenge, something funny, something you're into. No selling.
  • Wednesday or Thursday: A business post — money talk, what you learned, a behind-the-scenes, a setup. The story, not the product.
  • Friday or Saturday: A product post — a cup, a flavor, a party, the candy. This is the one that can be "book us."

The 10 posts that always work

  1. Count the cash box after a party. Say the real number.
  2. Update how much you still owe Dad for the machine.
  3. Rank all seven flavors worst to best. Disagree with each other.
  4. Invent a flavor that turns out terrible. Post it anyway.
  5. Argue about something ridiculous for 60 seconds.
  6. Set up a party, sped up to 10 seconds.
  7. Show what your setup kit actually contains.
  8. Close-up of candy going on cups, one at a time.
  9. A customer's kid tasting it for the first time (with permission!).
  10. Reply to a comment on camera.
The one rule that matters most: If every post is "book us!" the only followers are people who already want a rental — and once they've booked, they're done. The accounts that grow are the ones where people follow the PEOPLE. Half your posts should have nothing to do with slushies.

Every booking is content AND a referral opportunity. Don't leave without these.

At the party

  • Ask the host: "Can we take a few photos of the setup for our social media?" (Most say yes)
  • Shoot: the table setup, a cup close-up, the machine running, the line if there is one
  • If they say yes to video: 15 seconds of the party in action, a kid reacting to the candy
  • If they say no: respect it completely, take photos of JUST the machine/cups/candy, no people

After the party

  • Text the host a thank you: "Thanks for having us! Here's a photo from the setup. If any of your friends want to rent, our link is slushsisters.com/go/text"
  • Post one photo to Instagram within 24 hours while it's fresh
  • Note what worked and what didn't — this goes in the "what we tried" section of the marketing doc
