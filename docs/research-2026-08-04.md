# Research round two — the whiteboards and the teardowns

Written 2026-08-04, after the case review.

The case review was a debate between five analysts. This was different: five
**whiteboards** told to generate volume with no ranking and no filtering, plus
two **teardowns** of real creator accounts. The brief was explicit that the
previous round had been too timid — it treated one machine, one van, one unpaid
driver and one county as if they were physics, when they are a purchase order
and a hire.

Every board was also required to return an **unknowns register**: what nobody
knows, what it costs to find out, and which decision it is blocking. That
turned out to be the most valuable output of the round.

The kid-facing version of this is at `/read` — see
`docs/board/reading-room.js`.

---

## Part 1 — Three unresolved facts everything else rests on

These are not ideas. They are facts nobody has checked, that other analysis is
silently assuming. Under an hour combined, and two of them can invalidate other
work.

### 1. What is actually in the mix

**Nobody has read the label.** Every document in this repo argues about
ingredients — "bulk sweet-and-sour syrup," "corn syrup, citric acid,
artificial colour" — and not one names the product or quotes its ingredient
list.

That matters more than it looks, because of glycerol.

- A March 2025 case series in *Archives of Disease in Childhood* reviewed **21
  children aged 2 to 6¾** across the UK and Ireland who became acutely unwell
  after slush ice drinks: reduced consciousness or coma, critically low blood
  glucose, lactic acidosis. 93% were symptomatic within 60 minutes. All were
  hospitalised.
- The cause is **glycerol, used to maintain slush texture when sugar content is
  reduced.**
- In July 2025 the UK Food Standards Agency told retailers that slush drinks
  containing glycerol are **not suitable for children under 7**, that children
  7–10 should have no more than one 350ml serving a day, and asked them to stop
  free refills for under-10s. **The guidance explicitly extends to home slush
  kits.**

Set that against what this business physically is: an unattended, self-serve,
unlimited-refill frozen drink dispenser, left at a house full of
four-to-ten-year-olds, for four hours.

And it collides with `product-tiers.md`, which proposes a low-sugar and
zero-sugar tier and correctly warns that low sugar can freeze too hard. **The
industry's standard answer to exactly that problem is glycerol.** The freeze
test as currently designed points at the compound.

**Cost to resolve: $0 and twenty minutes.** Read the ingredient panel. Email
the supplier for a spec sheet.

**The asymmetry:** if the mix is glycerol-free and sugar-sweetened, that is the
strongest positioning asset this business has — *"we checked, here is the
label, here is what a national regulator says about the other kind."* If it is
not, they need to know before a journalist does.

Regardless of the answer, one unglamorous line belongs on the flavour card:
**"Little kids — one cup is plenty."**

### 2. Is the business legal at this address

Lakeway's code (Ch. 30 Art. 05) allows a **"no-impact home-based business" with
no permit** — but only where it generates no on-street parking or substantial
traffic, and **"none of its activities are visible from a street."** §30.05.004(5)
adds that no equipment or materials may be **"displayed or stored where visible
from anywhere off of the premises."** §30.05.004(7) prohibits selling alcohol
from a home-based business.

Machines in a garage. A van. Filming in the driveway. Every one of those is a
visibility question, and **the entire enforcement mechanism is one neighbour
filing one complaint** — which converts an automatic permit renewal into a full
hearing before the zoning and planning commission.

**First unknown inside the unknown: is the house even inside Lakeway city
limits?** Bee Cave and unincorporated Travis County are different regimes and
nobody has stated which applies.

Cost: one email to Lakeway Building & Development Services.
Blocks: machine #2, a van wrap, inventory storage, and every scale step after.

### 3. Is the van insured for what it is doing

A personal vehicle, loaded with commercial equipment, driven to a customer's
address, for money. **Personal auto policies commonly exclude business use, and
delivery of goods is the textbook excluded activity.**

The case review flagged the general liability call as the highest-value free
action available. **It missed this one, and it is a different policy and a
different phone call.** A liability gap costs the business; an auto gap costs
the family car, and it fires on an ordinary Saturday with no alcohol involved.

Also blocks hiring any driver — a hired operator in their own car creates
hired-and-non-owned auto exposure the business does not carry.

## Part 2 — What the whiteboards found

### Products and operations

- **The candy can be made, not bought.** Texas cottage food law explicitly
  covers candy — no permit, no inspection, up to $150,000/yr (SB 541, effective
  Sept 2025). That is the first answer anyone has produced to "a sweet on a rim
  is copyable in an afternoon" that survives the objection instead of dodging
  it. Worth a lawyer's eye before building on it.
- **The product step is four figures, not six.** Branded freeze pops run
  1,000–3,000 unit MOQs and 2,000–5,000 for custom flavour development, against
  50,000–200,000 for true private-label RTD. A full RTD run is ~$200K all-in.
  Short-run co-packers exist that will do 2–3 pallets.
- **Whole Foods runs a Local Producer Loan Program**, $1,000–$100,000,
  non-dilutive. It is what Mikaila Ulmer used, twelve miles away.
- **H-E-B's Quest for Texas Best** awards $100,000 across four prizes plus
  statewide shelf placement. The entry is a two-minute video.
- **Testing the concession thesis costs $52** — an Austin temporary food event
  permit ($200 multi-event). Kona Ice gives 20–25% back to host organisations
  and has returned $250M+ cumulatively; that giveback is *why* they get onto
  school property.
- **Machines:** a Donper double-bowl is ~$2,150, a Taylor SP-2 ~$2,773. Roughly
  nine rentals.
- **Scale 4 precedents:** BuzzBallz started 2009 as a Texas schoolteacher's
  master's thesis and sold to Sazerac in 2024 (est. $500M+). Bahama Buck's
  started in Lubbock, ~102 franchised units, ~$576K average unit volume.

### Brand and the anchor question

- **The Whataburger table tent is the closest precedent found.** A 25-cent
  plastic order-number marker, ~1.2 million replaced a year across ~815
  restaurants, stolen by Texans for graduation photos. Trivially copyable, and
  unmistakably one company's — because of a *number* and a *place*.
- **The mechanism is cost-to-imitate, not ability-to-imitate.** Refusals,
  standards, same-day work and a ritual performed at every party for five years
  are all copyable in principle and expensive in practice, because each one
  costs a rival revenue.
- **The candy is a retrieval cue, not an asset.** Its job is to make the cup
  photographable and the memory nameable. Copying the cue does not copy the name
  it retrieves.
- **The Garnish Bar** is the sharpest practical version: six bins, kids build
  their own rim. Copying a candy is a shopping trip; copying a second table with
  a job attached is a commitment a competitor with six machines will not make.
- **Two-layer naming.** Generic in public ("Mango" — what a parent says on the
  phone and types into search); house name on the card ("No. 4 — Harper's Tajín
  One") for voice.
- **A caveat worth keeping.** The widely cited "descriptive menu labels lift
  sales 27%" finding is Wansink, who resigned from Cornell in 2018 with 18
  papers retracted. Do not build a naming argument on it.
- **The dye evidence is real but not local.** FDA/HHS announced a phase-out
  initiative April 2025; Red No. 3 was revoked January 2025 with a 2027
  deadline; Consumer Reports (March 2026) puts ~72% of US adults at least
  somewhat concerned. But stated concern is not a parent declining a booking,
  and none of it is Austin or purchase-behaviour data. The hard part is
  concrete: **blue raspberry without Blue 1 probably cannot be blue, because
  blue raspberry is a format, not a fruit.**
- **The question nobody asked:** is there already a name for them locally? If
  people say "the slushie girls," that is the anchor — and it is a
  person-anchor, not a candy-anchor, and it was not chosen. One hour of
  searching the neighbourhood Facebook groups answers it.

### The recipe lab

The strongest single report of the round, because it overturns the assumption
the premium tier was built on.

**Freezing power depends on the number of dissolved molecules — molecular
weight — not on sweetness.** They are unrelated properties that only travel
together in ordinary sugar. Measured as PAC, sucrose = 100:

| | Freezing power | Sweetness |
|---|---|---|
| Sucrose | 100 | 100 |
| Glucose | 190 | 70 |
| Fructose (agave) | 190 | 170 |
| Allulose | ~190 | 70 |
| Salt | 585 | 0 |
| Alcohol | 740 | 0 |
| Sucralose | ~0 | ~60,000 |

Read it sideways: salt has zero sweetness and nearly six times sugar's
antifreeze power; sucralose is hundreds of times sweeter and does essentially
nothing to the freezing point.

So a stevia or monk-fruit build genuinely does produce a sweet block of ice.
**But agave is mostly fructose — you use less by weight and each gram is twice
the antifreeze.** Prediction: the agave and allulose versions stay slushy at
*lower* Brix than sugar does. If that holds, the premium product is not merely
possible, it is easier than expected — the opposite of what
`product-tiers.md` has been warning.

Other findings:

- **Three sources give three different target Brix ranges** (13–15, 13–17,
  21–24 for alcohol-free). They cannot all describe this machine. **Nobody has
  measured the mix already in the garage.** A handheld refractometer is $28–32
  and ten minutes, and every other number in the business depends on it.
- **Acid lets you cut perceived sweetness without touching Brix.** Citric acid
  raised the sucrose detection threshold from 0.424% to 0.624%. So you fix "too
  sweet" while staying in the safe freezing window. Better still: put the sour
  on the *rim*, where it cannot affect freezing at all.
- **Butterfly pea flower** steeps blue and turns pink-purple when acid hits it,
  in the cup. Zero dye, and it is the direct answer to the blue raspberry
  problem.
- **Anthocyanins** (hibiscus, black carrot, butterfly pea, purple sweet potato)
  are stable at low pH, and slush is acidic — the right pigment family. Beet is
  the wrong one; it wants pH 5–6 and fades in light. Colour retention is 91.2%
  at 4°C against 53.7% at 25°C, so a cold tank protects colour and a sunlit
  table does not.
- **An alcohol add-rate card.** The most likely way a rental "fails" is a
  customer pouring in a whole bottle; above ~10% ABV it will not freeze. A
  printed card protects the machine. Chill mode removes the ceiling entirely.
- **Dairy forces a daily clean** rather than roughly weekly. That is an
  operational decision, not a recipe one.
- **Equipment honesty:** the refractometer is the one purchase that matters.
  Skip the hydrometer (wrong tool for syrup). Lab glassware is theatre — *"if
  you buy it, buy it for the video, and say so out loud rather than pretending
  it is necessary."*

### Content

- **The Open Ledger has a real lineage and no child in it.** Buffer's public
  revenue dashboard, Baremetrics' open-startups initiative, the Open Startup
  List. An established genre with an established audience and no child-run
  example anywhere. *(Contested — see Part 4.)*
- **Austin is unusually dense for this.** Acton Children's Business Fair was
  founded in Austin in 2007 and runs **three separate fairs inside the service
  area** — Bee Cave, West Lake Hills, NW Austin/Cedar Park. Nobody aggregates
  that calendar.
- **The Mikaila Ulmer coverage is a map** of which outlets take this story: CBS
  Austin's *We Are Austin*, *KVUE Profiles*, Texas Standard, CultureMap Austin.
- **Two format inversions.** The vendor reviews the customer (a Party Report
  Card). The child is the employer (Dad's Performance Review) — the only
  version of this premise that is funny rather than cloying.
- **Email capture was framed wrong.** A parent planning a party does not want a
  newsletter; **they want to know when a Saturday opens up.** Date-based, not
  content-based, plus a Party Countdown series keyed to their event date.
- **`llms.txt` is not a ranking factor** per current reporting. Treat it as a
  courtesy map. Structured data does the actual work.
- **The core claim in `marketing.md` is untested.** That money content
  outperforms drink content is an assertion, not a finding. Ten posts, five of
  each, same weeks, settles it.
- **"One camera, one job."** A phone on a tripod at every setup by default.
  *Cadence problems are usually capture problems.*

### Wildcards, unknowns and time

- **Dollar utilisation is already 500%.** The rental industry benchmark for
  party and event equipment is ~150% — an item earning 1.5× its capital cost a
  year. Thirty rentals at $250 on a ~$1,500 machine is three times that.
  Numerical proof the machine was never the constraint.
- **Child-influencer law is further along than the case review said.**
  Minnesota, effective July 2025, prohibits under-14s from content-creation work
  outright. Tennessee's SB1469 passed 92–0 and 29–2 and bans under-14s from
  monetised content, effective July 2026. Both girls are under 14. If Texas
  follows, the core thesis becomes unavailable for three and six years
  respectively. **The hedge is to weight toward assets that survive a camera
  ban** — the ledger, the annual report, the standard, the product.
- **One risk scales with success.** The NYT analysed 5,000+ Instagram accounts
  of young girls run by their mothers across 2.1 million posts: male followers
  averaged 35% of the audience but **rose above 75% past 100,000 followers.**
  Meta removed ~135,000 accounts in 2025 for sexualised comments on
  adult-managed accounts featuring under-13s. **Put a gate in the plan now:**
  pull follower demographics at 10k, 25k and 50k and decide deliberately. Much
  easier to agree at zero than to argue at 60k.
- **Publish a right to delete.** Either girl can require any content taken down,
  any time, no reason needed. Minnesota's statute already contains it. Adopting
  it voluntarily costs nothing and it makes it *safe to say yes now*, because
  yes stops being permanent.
- **The time reframe, which inverts the case review.** The case review concluded
  the binding constraint is adult weekend hours and that child-hour paths
  self-terminate around fourteen. That is right for five years and wrong after.
  **At 16, Finley can drive — and Texas places no restriction on hours or times
  of day for 16- and 17-year-olds.** The scarcest input in the business becomes
  something the company owns rather than borrows. *This business is operating at
  its hardest possible moment right now.* Four things are worth more later and
  cannot be back-dated: the archive, the Open Ledger, the certification
  standard, and a book.

**Three lateral precedents worth stealing:**

- **Texas junior livestock auctions.** A grand champion steer sold for $675,000
  in 2025 and $1.5 million in 2026. Nobody paid that for beef. Texas has an
  institutionalised mechanism where adults with money pay an enormous premium
  *specifically because a child did the work.* → Auction one Saturday a year.
- **Lifetouch.** 25 million students a year from a model that is one recurring
  day per school; Shutterfly paid $825 million. → Own a *day* — Field Day, Last
  Day of School, Teacher Appreciation Week — across every LTISD elementary.
  Weekdays, no alcohol, girls can front it, renews annually without marketing.
- **Junior Achievement runs ~30,000 student mini-companies a year.** Don't build
  a chapter network from scratch — become the product one runs.

**And the model nobody had considered: placement, not rental.** Put a machine
somewhere permanently — a club pool, an HOA amenity centre, a sports complex —
and take a share of what it pours. Zero driving, zero setup, revenue Monday to
Friday. **The only model in any of these documents where money is decoupled from
adult weekend hours entirely.**

## Part 3 — The creator teardowns

### The correction

The seed observation — that @littleprintyco's "how we control humidity in our
3D printing room" post was a top performer, and therefore that unglamorous
operational detail beats polished product — **is false.**

| @littleprintyco post | Verified views |
|---|---|
| Serialised day-in-the-life, part 9 | **1.3M+** |
| Q&A / questions I get asked | 12.6K |
| Market day recap | 11.4K |
| Product ASMR | 7.6K |
| "Market booth 3D printed in-house" (process) | **3.9K** |
| Humidity control | in the same 3–7K cluster |

Technical process posts are among the **weakest** content on that account,
roughly 300× below the serial peak. What won was the child's routine. The
humidity post is memorable to a human reader; memorable is not performing.

*This is recorded because the original reading came from a screenshot and a
grid position, and was reported as a finding before it was checked.*

### What the adult teardown found instead

**It is not process — it is transformation.** The unit that performs is a
visible change of state with a beginning and an end. Filthy rug → clean rug.
Slab → table. Process *without* transformation — shop tours, workflow
explainers, equipment showcases — appears nowhere among the winners.

A slush machine has something a rug does not: **the product changes state on
camera in real time.**

And **specificity is the trust instrument** — the checkable, slightly boring
detail is evidence of competence a pretender could not fake. It converts at low
view counts, which matters more here than reach does.

### The finding that matters most

**Audience and revenue are almost completely decoupled.**

| Account | Audience | Actual revenue |
|---|---|---|
| @yourbincleaning | 117,500 followers; one reel at **1,709,577 likes** | **~$500 total**, 30–40 clients |
| @littleprintyco | 75,000+ IG, a 1.3M-view video | 712 Etsy sales in four months, ~$10K gross |

A 1.7-million-like video produced five hundred dollars. **Anyone planning around
"go viral → bookings" is planning around a mechanism this research found no
evidence for.**

The counterweight, from the adult side: Beardbrand's post-purchase surveys
showed **65% of customers first discovered them through YouTube while analytics
showed almost none.** Content converts, and the dashboard says it does not. The
only instrument that caught it was asking customers how they heard. But the same
company found its **highest-engagement content converted worst** — subscriber
count skyrocketed, sales did not follow.

### Serialisation, and the cheapest thing to get right

| | Trajectory |
|---|---|
| **@yourbincleaning** | "Day 2 of going door to door…" 6,959 likes → **"Day 7…" 1,709,577 likes** |
| **@littleprintyco** | part 9 = 1.3M → part 14 = 41.4K → **part 16 = 4.5K** |

One went up 245×, one collapsed 99.7%. The visible difference: **Ashton never
changed his title stem. Milla renamed her series four times mid-run.** Not proof
of causation — but it is free to get right and expensive to get wrong.

Corroborating from the adult side: Brandon Pleshek's **Spring Cleaning Spree**
began as a 30-videos-in-30-days dare, added a free checklist in year three, a
physical book in year four (500 copies, sold out in two days), and by 2025 was
doing **70 million views and $300,000 in monthly sales.** Nobody in either
teardown is winning with variety.

### Directly copyable

- **Put "parent-run" in the bio.** The highest-performing account in the study
  says it twice, unprompted. It defuses the exploitation objection before a
  commenter raises it and makes the account safe for aggregator reposts.
- **Film the noes.** The single best-performing content in the set is a child
  being politely turned down at a door. He cried the first time and that is in
  the press coverage. The freeze test is exactly this shape.
- **Story in the top link slot, shop second.**
- **Add the younger sister as the elder ages** — Koily Kurls did exactly this.

### Warnings

- **Pretty Paws Lemonade** grew from a neighbourhood stand to markets across Las
  Vegas, then got a **cease-and-desist from the health district after an
  anonymous complaint** and was shut down for operating without a licence. **The
  visibility caused the complaint.** This is the same tail risk the wildcard
  board reached from the Lakeway zoning code, independently.
- **Posting volume is not the variable.** One account posted 430 times for 3,400
  followers. Ashton got 117,500 from **27 posts**.
- **The aging-out answer.** Jonah Larson, viral at 11 in 2019, now ~17 with
  448,000 followers — a recent reel did 1,200 likes. Engagement collapsed while
  the number persisted. Every survivor did the same thing: **the business
  outgrew the child** and ended in manufacturing and retail. None stayed "kid
  content."
- **Read supplier PR as supplier PR.** The definitive @littleprintyco profile
  was written by Bambu Lab's Head of Public Relations.
- **Raw-beats-polished is vendor marketing.** Nearly every citation traces to
  agency blogs citing each other, with no independent study. What survives:
  audio present, face visible. *Spend nothing on gear; spend everything on what
  you point it at.*

### The number nobody has

> 118,000 global followers are worth close to nothing to a Lake Travis delivery
> radius.

**What share of the audience is within 30 miles?** That single ratio decides
whether this is a marketing channel or a media business, and no account studied
discloses it.

## Part 4 — Where the analysts disagree

Recorded rather than resolved, because both cases are strong.

**Is the Open Ledger the sharpest unclaimed position, or a bad trade?**

- *For:* a real lineage (Buffer, Baremetrics, the open-startup movement), an
  established audience, and no child-run example anywhere. It is also the audit
  standard a certification mark would need, and it cannot be back-dated.
- *Against:* BrewDog raised £75M from 200,000+ small investors on radical
  transparency and **collapsed in March 2026**. Everlane's per-item cost
  breakdowns became the criticism when they were judged selective. And: *"this
  is a children's business, and inviting scrutiny of a child's finances is a bad
  trade."*

Unresolved. It belongs to the convergent panel.

| | Unknown | Cost | Blocks |

| 1 | What is in the mix — glycerol or not | $0, 20 min | Fresh Press, freeze test, safety copy |

| 5 | Is "Slush Sisters" clear on the trademark register | $0 to search | Chapters, any product |
| 6 | Why did existing customers choose two kids | $0, ten texts | All positioning |
| 7 | Do customers remember the candy | $0, one evening | The entire anchor thesis |
| 8 | Is there already a local name for them | $0, one hour | Same |

| 10 | Brix of the current mix | $30, 10 min | Every cost and margin number |
| 11 | Cups actually poured per party | $0, six parties | Same |
| 12 | Does money content beat drink content | $0, ten posts | The core marketing claim |
| 13 | Would anyone pay more for Fresh Press | $0, five texts | The premium tier |
