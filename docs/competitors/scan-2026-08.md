# Competitor scan — Austin margarita / frozen-drink rentals (Aug 2026)

Mark pulled six competitor sites and asked what we can learn. This is the
teardown. It builds on `category-and-bar-program.md` (which already established
the "kids' tank" wedge and the price landscape) — read that first; this adds the
specific sites, the design lessons, and a prioritized action list.

The one-line version: **our positioning (two kids, the kids' tank, Fresh Press)
and — once the booking form works — our site are already ahead of this field.
The gaps that matter are booking, reviews, and a couple of conversion patterns,
not the product.**

## Site-by-site

### 1. Hill Country Margaritas — the design benchmark ("great looking," and it is)
Modern React build. **Full-bleed hero photo of the actual machine set up
poolside in the Hill Country at golden hour**, one headline ("Unlimited
Margaritas. Legendary Parties."), one amber CTA ("Reserve Margarita Machine"),
a **three-icon benefit row** (commercial-grade · unlimited refills · delivery &
pickup), then **flavor cards with mouth-watering drink photos**, a short FAQ,
and a 3-item nav. Booking is a flavor-picker flow.

- **Steal the structure:** one promise, one CTA, an icon "what's included" row,
  appetizing product cards, a tight FAQ. We already do most of this; the icon
  row and real drink photos are the gaps.
- **Do NOT steal the aesthetic.** It's the polished adult-liquor look
  `visual-identity.md` deliberately rejects — all adult, no kids, glossy. Our
  illustration-led, kid-run look is the differentiator, not a deficiency.
- Same alcohol model as us (customer adds their own — it's in their FAQ).

### 2. Margaritas of Austin — "terrible looking," and a lesson in spite of itself
A **Yahoo SiteBuilder 2.x table-layout relic** — image-button nav, no mobile,
reads abandoned. But the *offer* underneath is not nothing:
- **20+ flavors including non-alcoholic frozen drinks** — they already serve the
  no-alcohol crowd; they just bury it in a text wall instead of leading with it.
- Double & single units, "twice the choices for twice the fun."
- **No deposit — pay at delivery.**
- Deeply personal: "Jennifer Mathabela, Manager," a direct line, and a
  **"Margarita Emergency" number**. They even have a **Promise page** (so do we).
- **Lesson:** a good offer + human touch survives an awful site. We have a
  comparable offer, a *better* human story, and a good site — their weakness
  (design, mobile, no online booking) is our free win. Also validates that
  non-alcohol demand is real and that a "promise" page belongs in this category.

### 3. Leander Party Rentals — "offers more rentals," the one-stop shop
Full catalog: tables/chairs, bounce houses, tents, **concessions**, staging,
lighting, games, add-ons — with **real online booking** (date picker, cart,
live availability, delivery check). Concessions: popcorn $75, snow cone $60,
cotton candy $60–70, **frozen/margarita double-bowl machine $175**, Yeti $15.
- Their margarita machine is a **commodity afterthought** — no flavors, no
  candy, no experience, cheapest on the board.
- **Two lessons:** (a) **real booking with live date availability is table
  stakes** — they, Woolf's, and Hill Country all have transactional checkout;
  our form still fakes success (`site-audit.md`). This is the #1 gap. (b) A
  general party-rental company is a **referral channel**, not just a rival —
  they'd rather refer a margarita specialist than stock flavors and candy.

### 4. ATX Marg Rentals — closest positioning rival ($350, all-adult)
WordPress/Elementor, 2020-era, keyword-stuffed. Origin story nearly identical to
ours ("couldn't find a rental that met our needs, so we started one") — but ours
is stronger (two kids). Dual-chamber at $350, five flavors, **all adult**.
Confirms the `category-and-bar-program.md` finding: two tanks is table stakes;
**the kids' tank is the open lane.**

### 5. River City Frozen Beverages (margaritaequipment.com) — value + selection + proof
"Bargain Package **$224**," "NO ICE NEEDED," "no bartender needed," Georgetown/
Waco/Austin. Two bowls, **"largest mix selection in Central TX,"** and they also
sell **mixes, rim salts & sugars, gift cards, and machines to buy**. Big **review
wall** with named Google reviews ("Steve was great…").
- **Steal:** the **review wall** (their strongest asset), **gift cards** (a real
  revenue + gifting-season play), and note that **rim salts/sugars** is their
  version of a garnish upsell — ours (candy) is friendlier and more ownable.
- They compete on price/volume across a wide radius. We win on **hyper-local +
  premium + kids + design**, not price.

### 6. Woolf's Ritas — the lean, social-first operator (closest in spirit)
Square Online store, **Instagram-forward** (Mark included the IG link). Rental
"includes" is spelled out concretely: machine, cart, mixing jug, high-powered
cord, 1 mix, **40 cups, 40 straws**. Square checkout = CashApp/Apple Pay/cards.
- **Steal:** the **concrete "your rental includes" list** (removes doubt and
  quietly signals capacity — "40 cups"), **frictionless real payment**, and the
  **Instagram-as-front-door** model that `marketing.md` already bets on.

## What to actually do (prioritized)

1. **Fix the booking form so it works** *(already the #1 item in `site-audit.md`)*.
   Every serious competitor has real, transactional booking with live date
   availability. Ours displays success without submitting. Nothing else here
   matters as much. Options range from a form that actually emails the booking
   to a Square/checkout flow like Woolf's.
2. **Add a "what's included" icon row** to the home / booking / geo pages —
   delivery · setup · cups · candy garnish · next-morning pickup · the kids' tank.
   Hill Country and River City both make the inclusions scannable in seconds; we
   bury ours in prose.
3. **Spell out the rental like Woolf's** — a concrete "your $250 includes…" list
   with cup count, so the value vs the $175 bare box is obvious.
4. **Start collecting reviews from party one.** River City's review wall is its
   best asset and we have none yet (we're new). The two-sisters story will earn
   warm reviews; capture and display them. Biggest medium-term trust gap.
5. **Lean on the two moats nobody else has:** the **kids'/no-dye tank** and
   **Fresh Press** (fresh-squeezed, no artificial dye — zero competitors offer
   it). Keep these loud; they are the whole reason to pick us over a $224 box.
6. **Real drink + real-setup photos.** Hill Country's aspirational
   machine-at-the-party hero converts. This is the girls' phone shot list from
   `visual-identity.md` — pairs with, not replaces, the illustrations.
7. **Consider (dad calls):** gift cards (River City), no-deposit / pay-at-
   delivery (Margaritas of Austin), and a **referral relationship with a general
   party-rental firm** like Leander. None are urgent; all are cheap upside.

## What NOT to do
- Don't copy the glossy adult-cocktail look (Hill Country) — it erases our whole
  identity and trips the alcohol-brand trap in `visual-identity.md`.
- Don't compete on price against River City ($224) or Leander ($175). We are the
  premium-local-kids option, not the bargain box.
- Don't sprawl into a general rental catalog (Leander). Focus is the moat.
- Don't invent a category name — `category-and-bar-program.md` already settled
  this; keep the SEO pages matching "margarita machine rental austin."

### The correction that matters

Earlier docs (and Round 1) leaned on "nobody offers the kids' / no-alcohol
tank." **That's too strong.** Reading every site:

- **Margaritas of Austin** sells an explicit **"Just for Kids! Fiesta Slushee
  Package."**
- **TK** has a **"Something 4 The Kids"** section (church/school/family).
- **Margaritas To Go** markets mix "for adult beverages **as well as slushies
  for kids' parties**," with kid-coded flavors (bubblegum blue raspberry, cherry,
  dreamsicle), and makes the **same "pure cane sugar, no corn syrup"** argument
  our Fresh Press does.

So the honest wedge is **not** "we offer a non-alcohol option." Several do. Our
real, defensible differentiators are the **combination** nobody else has:

1. **Candy garnish on every single cup** — genuinely unique; the closest anyone
   comes is River City selling rim *salts*.
2. **We serve both markets without swaying** — two grown-up drinks, two with no
   alcohol, or one of each, always the customer's call. The no-alcohol option is
   **always available at no extra cost and never assumed or pushed** (our
   standing rule). Others either fill both tanks with adult drinks by default, or
   bury a "kids package" as an add-on you have to find. Note: we deliberately do
   **not** say "one tank is always the kids'" — that framing was pulled on
   purpose; it is an option we offer gladly, not a default we impose.
3. **Fresh Press: actually fresh-squeezed the morning of, zero artificial dye** —
   others tout "organic mix," but it's still bulk concentrate; **nobody squeezes
   limes.** Lead with "fresh-squeezed" and "no dye," not just "no corn syrup"
   (which Margarita Man already claims).
4. **It is really run by two kids** (Harper 11, Finley 8) — an origin no
   franchise or couple can copy.

Everything else (two tanks, non-alcohol available, delivery included, customer
brings alcohol) is table stakes in this market.
