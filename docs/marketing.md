# Marketing plan

The working strategy for Slush Sisters. This is a living document — update it as
things get tried, and record what did not work as well as what did. A plan
nobody edits is a plan nobody is using.

Companion file: [`content-ideas.md`](content-ideas.md) is the executable idea
bank, written for Finley and Harper to read and act on directly.

---

## 1. What we are actually building

The rentals pay for themselves and fund the operation. They are not the point.

**The asset is the girls.** Two sisters, 8 and 10, running a registered LLC with
real customers, real costs, and real money — that is a story that travels a very
long way, and it cannot be copied by a competitor with a bigger machine or a
lower price.

So every decision gets measured against: *does this make them more known and
more credible?* A booking that generates a great story is worth more than a
booking that does not.

---

## 2. The three channels, and their different jobs

People conflate these and then wonder why posting more does not produce
bookings. They do separate jobs.

### Instagram and TikTok — where "icon" gets built

Frequency, faces, personality. Nobody becomes a recognised local figure through
a website. Recognition comes from showing up repeatedly in feeds until people in
Lakeway feel like they know them.

This channel is for the **story**, not the product.

### The website — where strangers verify you are real

The page a reporter opens, a brand-partnership manager checks, a mom lands on
when a friend texts her the link. Social cannot do this job: a grid of Reels
does not tell someone the price, the service area, or whether this is a business
or a lemonade stand.

This channel is for **credibility and conversion**.

### Neighbourhood groups — where the bookings actually come from

This is the one most people miss, and for a kids' party rental it out-performs
both of the above.

Facebook neighbourhood groups, HOA newsletters, school parent listservs,
Nextdoor. That is where *"does anyone know someone who does frozen drinks for a
birthday?"* gets asked, in Rough Hollow and Belterra and Steiner, every week.

A viral TikTok reaches teenagers in Ohio. A reply in the Bee Cave moms group
reaches the person planning a party in three weeks.

The six service-area pages exist largely so that reply lands well — when someone
drops the link in the Belterra group, they get a page that names Belterra.

## 3. Campaigns worth running

### 3.1 The organisation giveaway — highest ROI on the list

Give a rental away free to something local that has a mailing list. Teacher
appreciation day. A Little League end-of-season party. A school carnival. The
fire station.

- **Cost:** one batch of mix.
- **Return:** photos of the girls serving a crowd, a story worth telling, and —
  the actual point — the *organisation* shares it to several hundred local
  parents with their own credibility attached.

That is a warmer introduction than any ad, and it seeds exactly the
neighbourhoods being served. Two or three of these in a season builds local
recognition that money cannot buy at this scale.

**Do this first.**

### 3.2 Lake Austin seeding — different maths entirely

Everywhere else a booking is worth $250. In Westlake, Rob Roy and Davenport
Ranch a booking is worth $250 *plus* exposure to a room of ideal customers,
hosted by someone whose recommendation carries weight.

Treat it accordingly:

- **Overdeliver on purpose.** Extra garnish, arrive early, never rush the
  pickup. Costs a few dollars, returns a host who tells ten families.
- **The girls must be physically there at setup.** This is the whole thing. A
  dad dropping off a machine is a rental company and is forgettable. Two kids in
  matching shirts explaining how it works is a story the host retells all night
  to every guest who walks in. That retelling *is* the marketing, and it does
  not happen if the setup is done efficiently by an adult.
- **Ask for the referral there specifically.** No code, no discount — just "if
  you know someone." Affluent parents refer readily when the story is good and
  nothing transactional is attached.

**The deliberate version:** pick three to five high-reach households — people
who actually post, not merely people with money. Offer the first rental free or
half price. The only ask is one tagged story.

This works better than normal influencer seeding because nobody feels like they
are shilling. Posting *"two sisters, 8 and 10, run this whole business and came
to set up my kid's party"* makes the poster look good. It reads as a discovery,
not an ad — the rarest quality in influencer content, obtained free because the
story is true.

> **Constraint that matters:** high-profile households are privacy-sensitive.
> Photos of their house, their kids, or their guests are usually a hard no. Plan
> for content from those parties to be the girls only — no house, no guests, no
> address. Ask before every shot. Getting this wrong once closes that market.

### 3.3 Community-named flavour

Let local kids vote on and name a new flavour. "Lake Travis Blue."

Every voter becomes invested, and it gives a school or neighbourhood group a
reason to share the poll — which gets the brand into those groups without it
being an ad.

### 3.4 Cross-referral with adjacent vendors

Bounce houses, party planners, kids' photographers, balloon arch people. Same
party, same customer, zero overlap. One conversation each, no ad spend, warm
introductions both directions.

Start with bounce house companies — the overlap is nearly total.

### 3.5 Local press

This story is exactly the format these outlets run:

| Outlet | Why |
| --- | --- |
| **Community Impact** | Covers Lake Travis and Westlake specifically. Local-business focus |
| **KVUE / KXAN** | Both run kid-entrepreneur segments |
| **Austin Monthly** | Loves an Austin-flavoured human story |
| **Lake Travis View** | Hyper-local, exactly the service area |
| **Austin American-Statesman** | Longer shot, bigger reach |

Free, high-credibility, and permanent in search results. This is what the
`/press` page exists to serve — it lets a reporter say yes without a phone call.

### 3.6 Neighbourhood referral

In Steiner or Belterra, one party seeds five — the same families see each other
constantly. Something simple for a referral that books. Keep it easy to explain
in one sentence.

## 5. What we are measuring

Currently: **nothing.** There is no analytics on the site, which means there is
no baseline for any of this.

Worth fixing before running campaigns, so results are attributable:

- Cloudflare Web Analytics — free, no cookie banner, one snippet
- Google Business Profile insights — how people find the listing
- Simply asking every booker "how did you hear about us" and writing it down.
  For a business at this volume that hand-kept list beats any dashboard.

## 6. What not to bother with

- **Paid ads.** Pointless at this volume. The money is better spent on a free
  giveaway rental that generates a story.
- **Posting daily for its own sake.** Two good posts a week beats seven filler
  ones, and filler is what burns kids out on the whole thing.
- **Chasing virality.** Not a strategy. The compounding asset is local
  recognition and a body of work, not one lucky video.
- **Discount codes.** Undercuts a flat honest price that is already a selling
  point.

---

## 7. Open decisions

- **How identifiable do the girls get?** First names and faces is one level.
  School, neighbourhood, and a regular schedule is another. Press coverage is
  permanent and searchable. Worth deciding deliberately rather than drifting.
- **Who runs the accounts?** Platform minimum age is 13 and both girls are under
  it, so the accounts have to be parent-operated. Saying so plainly in the bio
  is both honest and what protects the accounts from removal later.
- **The handles are not secured.** `@slushsisters` on Instagram currently
  belongs to an unrelated account. Everything in this document depends on the
  name. This is the most urgent item here and it costs nothing.
