# The Slush Sisters playbook

Strategy and ideas in one place, on purpose. Finley and Harper should read the
strategy too, not just the task list — understanding *why* something works is
the part that lasts after this particular business is over.

Keep it updated. Record what did not work as well as what did. A plan nobody
edits is a plan nobody is using.

---

## Part 1 — What we are actually building

The rentals pay for themselves and fund everything. They are not the point.

**The asset is the girls.** Two sisters, 8 and 10, running a registered LLC with
real customers, real costs and real money. That story travels a very long way,
and no competitor can copy it with a bigger machine or a lower price.

So every decision gets measured against one question: *does this make them more
known and more trusted?* A booking that produces a great story is worth more
than a booking that does not.

## Part 2 — The three places people find you

These do different jobs. People mix them up and then wonder why posting more
does not produce bookings.

### Instagram and TikTok — where people get to know you

Faces, personality, showing up over and over until people in Lakeway feel like
they already know you. Nobody becomes a recognised local figure through a
website.

**This is for the story, not the product.**

### The website — where a stranger checks you are real

The page a reporter opens, a brand checks, a mom lands on when a friend texts
her your link. Social cannot do this job: a wall of videos does not tell someone
the price, the service area, or whether this is a real business or a lemonade
stand.

**This is for proof.**

### Neighbourhood Facebook groups — where the bookings actually come from

This is the one nearly everyone misses, and for a kids' party rental it beats
both of the above.

Neighbourhood Facebook groups, HOA newsletters, school parent emails, Nextdoor.
That is where *"does anyone know someone who does frozen drinks for a
birthday?"* gets asked, in Rough Hollow and Belterra and Steiner, every single
week.

A video that goes viral reaches teenagers in Ohio. A reply in the Bee Cave moms
group reaches the person planning a party in three weeks. One of those pays the
bills.

The six service-area pages on the site exist mostly so that reply lands well —
when someone drops the link in the Belterra group, they get a page that names
Belterra.

## Part 3 — The one big secret about what to post

Most people get this wrong, so here is the head start.

**People do not share videos about slushies. They share videos about two kids
running a real business.**

A video of a nice drink gets a like. A video of you counting the money you made
and working out how much you still owe Dad for the machine gets *sent to someone
else*. That is the entire difference.

When you are stuck: **show the business, not the drink.**

---

## Part 4 — But it cannot all be selling

Here is the trap. If every single post is about slushies, the only people who
follow you are people who want to rent a slushie machine. That is a small group,
and once they have booked, they are done.

The accounts that actually grow do something different: **most of what they post
has nothing to do with what they sell.** People follow *people*. Then, later,
some of those people need a frozen drink machine — and by then you are not a
rental company to them, you are two girls they like.

A rough shape to aim for:

| Roughly how much | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| Half | You two — funny, real, trends, being sisters |
| A third | The business — money, setups, what you are learning |
| The rest | The product — flavours, the machine, the candy |

Most businesses invert that and wonder why nobody follows them.

### Things to post that are not about slushies at all

- Arguing about something ridiculous and refusing to back down
- Your inside jokes
- Things you are actually into that have nothing to do with the business
- Summer in Texas — the heat, the lake, the pool
- Sibling challenges. Who is faster, who is stronger, who is right
- Reacting to other kids who run businesses
- Answering questions people ask in the comments
- Being bored in the van on a long drive
- Something you are learning that is hard
- Your dog, if she cooperates

None of that sells anything. All of it builds the thing that eventually sells
everything.

## Part 5 — How to ride a trend

Trends are **borrowed attention**. When a sound or a format is trending, the app
is already pushing it to people. If you use it, you get carried along with it.
You do not have to invent anything to be noticed — you have to be recognisable
inside something people already like.

This is the fastest way to reach people who have never heard of you, and the
product does not need to appear at all.

### Finding them

- **The sounds.** On TikTok, tap the sound on any video to see how many others
  have used it. Rising fast, not yet enormous, is the sweet spot.
- **Your own For You page.** If you see the same format three times in two days,
  that is a trend. Your feed is free research.
- **Other small businesses.** Watch what bakeries, florists and dog groomers are
  doing. Small local businesses adapt trends earlier than big brands.
- **Save everything.** When you spot one, save the video immediately. You will
  not remember it later.

### Using them

1. **Speed matters more than quality.** A trend is usually dead in a week or
   two. A rough video posted on day three beats a perfect one posted on day
   twelve. If you are late, skip it — there will be another one.
2. **Use the actual sound.** On TikTok the sound is most of the reach. Adding
   your own music to a trending format throws away the point.
3. **Do not copy — bend it.** The question is always: *what is the Slush Sisters
   version of this?* Same format, your life inside it. That is what makes it
   yours instead of one of ten thousand identical posts.
4. **Text on screen from the first frame.** Most people watch with sound off
   until something makes them turn it on.
5. **Two of you is an advantage.** Loads of trends need two people. Most
   accounts do not have that and have to film alone.

### Formats that come back around forever

Specific trends die. These shapes keep returning under new names, so learn the
shape and you will spot the trend when it arrives:

- **Ranking or tier list** — put things in order, argue about it
- **"Things nobody tells you about ___"** — the honest-experience format
- **"Tell me you're ___ without telling me you're ___"**
- **Day in the life** — the whole day, sped up
- **Get ready with me** — talking while doing something else
- **POV** — "POV: you booked the Slush Sisters"
- **Before and after / transition** — a mess becomes a set-up party
- **This or that** — two options, pick fast
- **Replying to a comment on camera** — free content, and people love being
  answered
- **Storytime** — something that actually happened, told properly
- **Expectation vs reality** — what you planned, what happened
- **Duet or stitch** — react to someone else's video

### Two rules

**Never use a sound without listening to the whole thing.** Trending sounds are
often clips of songs with words you would not want attached to your business.
Listen all the way through, every time.

**If a trend needs you to be mean, be unsafe, or pretend something untrue,
skip it.** There is always another trend. There is only one reputation.

## Part 7 — What makes a post good

- **Show it, do not describe it.** Filming the machine pouring beats saying it
  pours well.
- **The first two seconds decide everything.** Start at the interesting bit, not
  at "hi guys."
- **Real beats perfect.** A spill you laugh about does better than a flawless
  shot. People can tell, and they prefer the real one.
- **One idea per video.**
- **Talk like yourselves.** You are 10 and 8 and that is the entire appeal. Do
  not try to sound like an advert.

## Part 8 — Rules

Short list, and these ones actually matter.

1. **Never film someone's house, their kids, or their address** without asking a
   grown-up in that family first. Some people care enormously. Asking costs
   nothing; getting it wrong loses a customer permanently.
2. **No street names, no house numbers, no school name in a caption.**
3. **Never say where you will be at a specific time in the future.** Afterwards
   is fine.
4. If a customer says no filming, that is a no. Do not ask twice.
5. Listen to a trending sound all the way through before using it.
6. If something feels weird, stop and ask Mom or Dad. You will never be in
   trouble for stopping.

## Part 9 — Campaigns for the grown-ups to set up

The girls execute these; someone has to make the phone call first.

### The organisation giveaway — highest return of anything here

Give a rental away free to something local with a mailing list. Teacher
appreciation day. Little League end-of-season. A school carnival. The fire
station.

- **Cost:** one batch of mix.
- **Return:** the *organisation* shares it to several hundred local parents with
  their own credibility attached.

Warmer than any advert, and it seeds exactly the neighbourhoods being served.
Two or three a season builds recognition money cannot buy at this scale. **Do
this first.**

### Lake Austin seeding — different maths

Everywhere else a booking is worth $250. In Westlake, Rob Roy and Davenport
Ranch it is worth $250 *plus* a room full of ideal customers, hosted by someone
whose recommendation carries weight.

- **Overdeliver on purpose.** Extra garnish, arrive early, never rush pickup.
- **The girls must be there at setup.** A dad dropping off a machine is a rental
  company and is forgettable. Two kids explaining how it works is a story the
  host retells all night to every guest. That retelling *is* the marketing, and
  it does not happen if an adult does the setup efficiently.
- **Ask for the referral there specifically.** No code, no discount, just "if
  you know someone."

**Deliberate version:** pick three to five households who actually post. Offer
the first rental free or half price. The only ask is one tagged story. It works
because posting *"two sisters, 8 and 10, run this whole business"* makes the
poster look good — a discovery, not an advert.

> **Constraint:** these households are privacy-sensitive. Photos of their house,
> kids or guests are usually a hard no. Plan for content from those parties to
> be the girls only. Ask before every shot. Getting it wrong once closes that
> market.

### Cross-referral

Bounce houses, party planners, kids' photographers, balloon arches. Same party,
same customer, no overlap. One conversation each. Start with bounce houses — the
overlap is nearly total.

### Local press

| Outlet | Why |
| --- | --- |
| **Community Impact** | Covers Lake Travis and Westlake specifically |
| **KVUE / KXAN** | Both run kid-entrepreneur segments |
| **Austin Monthly** | Loves an Austin human story |
| **Lake Travis View** | Hyper-local, exactly the service area |

Free, credible, and permanent in search. This is what a `/press` page exists to
serve — it lets a reporter say yes without a phone call.

## Part 11 — What not to bother with

- **Paid ads.** Pointless at this volume. Better spent on a free giveaway rental
  that produces a story.
- **Posting every day just to post.** Two good ones a week beats seven filler
  ones, and filler is what makes people quit.
- **Chasing virality.** Not a strategy. The thing that compounds is local
  recognition and a body of work, not one lucky video.
- **Discount codes.** Undercuts a flat honest price that is already a selling
  point.

## Part 12 — Measuring it

Currently **nothing**, which means no baseline for any of this. Worth fixing
before running campaigns:

- Cloudflare Web Analytics — free, no cookie banner, one snippet
- Google Business Profile insights
- Asking every booker "how did you hear about us" and writing it down. At this
  volume that hand-kept list beats any dashboard.

## Part 13 — Open decisions

- **How identifiable do the girls get?** First names and faces is one level.
  School, neighbourhood and a routine is another. Press coverage is permanent
  and searchable.
- **Who runs the accounts?** Platform minimum is 13 and both are under it, so
  they must be parent-operated. Saying so in the bio is honest and protects the
  accounts from removal.
- **The handles are not secured.** `@slushsisters` on Instagram belongs to
  someone else. Everything here depends on the name. Most urgent item in this
  document, and it costs nothing.
